Details Behind the Polybius Cipher

What is the Polybius Cipher?

The Polybius cipher is a cipher where each letter of the alphabet (except I and J, which are combined) is written into a 5x5 grid (also known as a Polybius square), in alphabetical order, by row. The encryption principle works by locating the letter's row and column coordinate, turning that letter into numbers. The decryption principle works by locating the letter’s row and column coordinate, turning it back into its original letter. Though there are many variations, where a key is used, and the letters of the key are recorded first, within the Polybius square, or alphabets are just scrambled.

How to Make The Polybius Cipher Stronger

As noted earlier, there are many versions of the Polybius Cipher. To enhance its security and prevent easy decryption, you can scramble the alphabet or use an optional key at the beginning of the grid. These methods make it much harder for an average user to decipher the ciphertext. This cipher should by no means ever be used in modern applications.
